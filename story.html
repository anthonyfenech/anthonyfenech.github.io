The Last Game at Tiger Stadium

Fifteen years of memories, one final goodbye, and the stories that never made the headlines

By Anthony Fenech

Detroit Free Press | December 5, 2025

The lights at Comerica Park flickered on for the last time that September evening, casting long shadows across the infield dirt. I had covered hundreds of games by then, but this one felt different. This one felt final.

In the press box, veteran reporters who had seen it all sat in unusual silence. Below us, fans filed in slowly, savoring every moment, knowing that baseball—real baseball, the kind that lives in memory—was about to become history.

This is the story of that night. But more than that, it's the story of fifteen years spent chronicling a team, a city, and a game that refuses to let go of those who love it most.

The press box at Comerica Park during the final game of the 2025 season. Veteran reporters reflected on decades of covering the Tigers. (Photo by Staff Photographer)

Miguel Cabrera once told me something I've never forgotten. We were standing near the batting cage on a quiet Tuesday afternoon in April, and he looked out at the empty seats and said, "Every game is somebody's first game. Every game is somebody's last game."

At the time, I wrote it down in my notebook and moved on to the next question. But years later, those words echo differently. They carry weight now.

• • •

The clubhouse after a loss is a particular kind of quiet. It's not peaceful—it's heavy, thick with disappointment and unspoken questions. But on this night, even in defeat, there was something else in the air. Relief, maybe. Or gratitude.

I watched as players cleaned out their lockers, some for the last time. Equipment managers packed away jerseys that would never be worn again. Coaches exchanged handshakes that lasted a beat too long.

"Every game is somebody's first game. Every game is somebody's last game."

In my fifteen years covering this team, I've learned that the best stories aren't found in post-game quotes or box scores. They're found in the moments between pitches, in the conversations that happen when the cameras are off, in the humanity that exists beneath the uniform.

Players gathered in the clubhouse following the season finale, reflecting on a year that tested their resilience and resolve. (Photo by Staff Photographer)

Baseball is a game of numbers, but it's also a game of stories. And after fifteen years, I've collected more stories than statistics. Stories about rookies finding their way, veterans hanging on for one more season, and the people behind the scenes who make it all work.

• • •

As I walked out of the stadium that final night, my phone buzzed with the inevitable question: "What's next?" But for once, I didn't have an answer ready. Sometimes you have to let a story end before you can figure out where the next one begins.

What I do know is this: The game will go on. The season will turn. And somewhere, right now, a young reporter is walking into their first press box, notebook in hand, ready to fall in love with this beautiful, maddening, unforgettable game.

Just like I did, fifteen years ago.
